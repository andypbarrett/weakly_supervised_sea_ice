4  Data

4.1 Overview

This section provides an overview of satellite instruments and data commonly used to estimate sea ice variables. Visible, near-infrared, and microwave sensors are used. Table Table 4.1 shows the wavebands for these sensors. However, because the polar regions are dark for part of the year and also the polar regions tend to be cloudy, microwave wavelengths are often preferred because they can operate during polar night and the atmosphere is largely transparent at these wavelengths (Figure 4.1).

Remote sensing instruments are either passive or active sensors. Passive sensors measure radiation from an external light source (e.g. the Sun or Earth) either reflected, scattered or emitted from a surface. Active sensors provide their own source of radiation and measure the reflected or backscattered signal.

Here we use focus on active sensors first, in particular Synthetic Aperture Radar.

4.2.2 Sentinel-1

4.2.2.1 Overview

Sentinel-1 is a C-Band radar. The Sentinel-1 mission was planned as a two-satellite constellation, with the two satellites sharing the same orbit plane. Sentinel-1A was launched on 3 April 2014. Sentinel-1B was launched 25 April 2016. Sentinel-1B was retired on 23 December 2021 because of a power supply issue. Sentinel-1C was launched 5 December 2024 and Sentinel-1D was launched November 2025.

4.2.2.2 Instrument Characteristics

Orbit: Sun synchronous near-polar orbit with a \(98.18^\circ\) inclination.

Altitude: 693 km

Revisit Time: 6 to 12 days depending on regions

Band: C-Band with central frequency 5.405 GHz

Channels: Depends on mode (see Table 4.2)

Extra Wide Swath mode is the primary mode for sea ice monitoring providing data in HH and HV polarizations. Images are distributed as Ground Range Detected products. Most sea ice regions only have medium resolution images available. Images are constructed from three swaths using the Terrain Observation with Progressive Scanning SAR (TOPSAR) imaging method.

Products:

  • Level 1 Single Look Complex data: complex images of phase and amplitude
  • Level 1 Ground Range Detected: multi-looked intensity
  • Level 2 Ocean (OCN) data: ocean geophysical parameters

4.2.2.3 File Format

Products are distributed in SAFE format. Each product (“image”) contains a measurement dataset in GeoTIFF. Calibration and other supporting data are in XML format.

4.3.1 Overview

Passive microwave sensors measure electromagnetic radiation in the microwave region that is emitted by the Earth.

Daily brightness temperature product produced from the Japanses Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) Advanced Microwave Scanning Radiometer 2 (AMSR2) instrument.

Channels: 18.7 GHz vertical (v) and horizontal (h), 23.8 GHz (v), and 36.5 GHz (v & h).

Grid Cell Size: nominally 25 km

Actual Resolution is dependent on frequency

4.3.3 DMSP SSM/I and SSMIS Daily Polar Gridded Brightness Temperatures

Daily brightness temperature product produced from orbital swath data from the Special Sensor Microwave/Imager (SSM/I) aboard the Defense Meteorological Program (DMSP) F8, F11 and F13 platforms, and the Special Sensor Microwave Imager/Sounder (SSMIS) aboard DMSP F17 and F18.

Channels: 19.3 GHz vertical (v) and horizontal (h), 22.2 GHZ (v), 37.0 GHz (v & h), 85.5 GHz (v & h) on SSM/I, and 91.7 GHz (v & h) on SSMIS.

Grid Cell Size: 85.5 GHz and 91.7 GHz channel data are gridded to 12.5 km cells, all other channels are gridded to 25 km.

Actual resolutions depend of frequency. Footprint are elliptical.

Frequency (GHz) Resolution (km)
19.3 45 x 70
37.0 30 x 38
85.0 14 x 16

Coverage: North and South Hemisphere

Grid CRS: NSIDC Polar Stereographic North and South (EPSG:3411, EPSG:3412)

Resampling method: A simple drop-in-the-bucket averaging.

Time Period: 1978 to present

4.3.4 Calibrated Enhanced-Resolution Passive Microwave Daily EASE-Grid 2.0 Brightness Temperature ESDR

An enhanced-resolution, multi-sensor brightness temperature product from passive microwave sensors. Sensors are:

  • Scanning Multichannel Microwave Radiometer (SMMR) on NIMBUS-7
  • Special Sensor Microwave/Imager (SSM/I) on DMSP 5D-2/F8, F10, F11, F13, F14, DMSP 5D-3/F15
  • Special Sensor Microwave Imager/Sounder (SSMIS) on DMSP 5D-3/F16, F17, F18, F19
  • Advanced Microwave Scanning Radiometer - Earth Observing System (AMSR-E) on AQUA
  • Advanced Microwave Scanning Radiometer 2 (AMSR2) on GCOM-W1

Channels: multiple, sensor dependent but between 6.6 and 92 GHz

Grid Cell Size: 25 km (All), 3.125 km (> 30 GHz), 6.25 km (12 GHz to 30 GHz), 12.5 km (< 12 GHz)

Resampling Method: 25 km uses drop-in-the-bucket average, enhanced resultions use Radiometer Version Scatterometer Image Reconstruction (rSIR)

Time Period: 1987 to Present depending on sensor (AMSR2 and SSMIS F16, F17, F18 all operational)

Temporal Frequency: Twice Daily

4.4 Visible Imagery

4.4.1 MODIS

The MODIS instrument on-board Terra and Aqua Satellites has MODIS Level 1B Calibrated Radiances for the 36 MODIS bands and reflectances for solar refelectance bands 1 through 19 and 26.

The MODIS sea ice algorithm uses the following bands from MOD02 MODIS Level 1B Calibrated and Geolocated Radiances. The sea ice algorithm uses the MODIS Land/Water mask, Solar zenith angle, geolocation, and Geoid height from MOD03, and the Unobstructed field of view and day/night glags from MOD35_L2 products.

There are three MOD02 products, each product containing bands for one of the three spatial resolutions.

MOD02 Product Resolution (m) Bands
MOD02QKM 250 1, 2
MOD02HKM 500 3-7
MOD021KM 1000 8-36
Band Central \(\lambda\) (\(\mu m\)) Lower \(\lambda\) (\(\mu m\)) Upper \(\lambda\) (\(\mu m\)) Resolution (m) Purpose
1 0.645 0.620 0.670 250 Threshold
2 0.865 0.841 0.876 250 Threshold
4 0.555 0.545 0.565 500 NDSI
6 1.640 1.628 1.652 500 NDSI
31 11.03 10.780 11.280 1000 IST
32 12.02 11.770 12.270 1000 IST

Sea ice is assumed to be snow covered and is mapped based on a normalized difference of surface reflectances between bands 4 and 6, the Normalized Difference Snow Index.

\[ NDSI = \frac{band4 - band6}{band4 + band6} \]

Pixels are identified as sea ice if the following criteria pass.

  • \(NDSI > 4\)
  • \(band2 > 0.11\)
  • \(band1 > 0.10\)

An Ice Surface Temperature (IST) product is also calculated. Surface radiances from bands 31 and 32 are first converted to brightness temperatures using a version of the Planck function.

\[ T_{\lambda} = \frac{c_2 \nu}{ln\left(1 + \left(\frac{e c_2 \nu^3}{E}\right)\right)} \]

where \(T_{\lambda}\) is brightness temperature in \(\mathrm{K}\), \(\nu\) is central wavelength in \(\mathrm{cm^{-1}}\), E is radiance from the sensor in \(\mathrm{m\,W m^{-2}\,sr\,cm^{-4}}\), \(e\) is emmissivity, \(c_1\) is \(1.910659 \times 10^5\: \mathrm{m\, W m^{-2}\, sr\, cm^{-4}}\), and \(c_2\) is \(1.438833\: \mathrm{cm\, K}\).

4.4.3 Sentinel-2

Sentinel-2 is a multi-spectral imager.

Level 1C and Level 2A granules are 110 km by 110 km geometrically corrected ortho-images projected onto a Universal Transverse Mercator (UTM) CRS. Geometric corrections remove distortions from surface topography and imaging systems. Granules are designed to overlap.

Level 1C data are top-of-atmosphere reflectances. These data are based on Level 1B products that have been radiometrically corrected. Level 1C products include cloud masks and other QA grids.

Level 2A products are atmospherically corrected surface reflectance products derived from Level 1C products. In addition to surface reflectance, level 2A products include a Aerosol Optical Thickness (AOT) map, a Water Vapour (WV) map and a Scene Classification (SCL) map.

Grid size: 10 m, 20 m or 60 m depending on spectral band.

Documentation: Sentinel-2 Products

4.4.4 Sentinel-3

The main objective of the Sentinel-3 mission is to measure sea surface topography with the Altimetry Surface Topography Mission (STM), sea and land surface temperature with the Sea and Land Surface Temperature Radiometer (SLSTR), and ocean and land surface color with the Ocean and Land Colour Instrument (OLCI)..

Istomina, Niehaus, and Spreen (2025) used OLCI and SLSTR channels to map melt ponds.

4.4.4.1 SLSTR

Channels: 2 channels in the visible (555 nm, 660 nm), a near-infrared channel (870 nm) and six thermal infrared channels (1370 to 12000 nm).

Swath width: 1470 km for nadir swath scanner and 740 km for oblique swath scanner.

Grid Size and Resolution: Nominal resolution of 500 m.

Temporal frequency: mean global revisit time is 1.9 days. For 3A and 3B the revisit time is 0.9 days at the equator.

4.4.4.2 OLCI

Channels: 12 channels in the visible wavelengths spanning 400 nm to 753.75 nm, and 9 channels in the near infrared spanning 761.25 nm to 1020 nm.

Swath Width: 1270 km

Grid Size and Resolution: the OLCI instrument has a 68.5\(^\circ\) field of view. The nominal spatial resolution is 300m

Temporal frequency: Orbital cycle is 27 days. Revisit time is 2-3 days.
